Three Legal Pathways for Registering Property in a Child's Name

Depending on whether your child is a minor or an adult at the time of purchase at Ganga County, you can adopt one of three primary legal mechanisms:

1. Direct Purchase as Minor (Natural Guardian)

You can purchase the plot in your child's name while they are a minor. The sale deed will explicitly state that the transaction is executed through you as their natural guardian (father or mother) until they turn 18.

2. Purchase & Subsequent Gift Deed

Parents buy and register the plot in their own name first, enjoying complete operational flexibility. Later, through a registered Gift Deed, ownership is transferred to the child with tax exemptions.

3. Joint Ownership (Parent & Adult Child)

If your child is an adult, registering as joint co-owners (e.g., Father & Son/Daughter) facilitates seamless succession, easier bank financing, and shared estate management.

Frequently Asked Questions on Minor Property Ownership & Succession

Get expert answers to common legal inquiries regarding property registration and inheritance in Uttar Pradesh:

Can a plot be legally registered directly in the name of a minor child in Uttar Pradesh?

Yes, under Indian property law, a minor child can legally own immovable property. However, since a minor cannot enter into a binding contract, the property must be purchased and registered through a natural guardian (father or mother) or a court-appointed guardian until the child attains 18 years of age.

What are the tax implications when gifting a plot to a child in India?

Under Section 56(2) of the Income Tax Act, gifts made to 'lineal descendants' (such as children) are completely exempt from income tax in the hands of the recipient, regardless of the property's market value. However, stamp duty and registration fees still apply during the execution of the gift deed.
